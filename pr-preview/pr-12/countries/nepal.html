Historical Background

Christianity had almost no foothold in the Hindu kingdom of Nepal until the country opened up in the mid-20th century, and proselytism was long a criminal offense. The end of the Hindu monarchy and the 2015 secular constitution coincided with rapid church growth, but anti-conversion provisions carried over and continue to constrain Christians.

Sources: (2023) U.S. State Department IRF Report 2023 - Nepal; (2026) Open Doors World Watch List 2026

Modern-Day Situation

Nepal's constitution declares the country secular but prohibits religious conversion, and the criminal code bans both proselytism and 'hurting religious sentiment.' Pastors and Christian converts have been arrested and prosecuted; documented cases include a pastor jailed over cow slaughter and preachers charged for evangelism, leaving the fast-growing but small Christian minority legally vulnerable.

Sources: (2023) U.S. State Department IRF Report 2023 - Nepal
